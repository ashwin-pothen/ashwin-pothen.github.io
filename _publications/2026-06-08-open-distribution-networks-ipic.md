This paper presents a multi-agent stress-testing framework comparing two distribution architectures under compound disruptions: a traditional network with three privately operated distribution centers, and a hyperconnected Physical Internet network accessing thirteen shared Open Certified Storage Centers (OCSCs) through PI lane-tariff pricing and annual capacity reservation. The simulator integrates weekly inventory positioning, production planning, raw-material procurement, and daily customer fulfillment with willingness-to-wait acceptance across a stress-test library of 341 peak-timing scenarios, executed with five random seeds for 1,710 simulation runs. At baseline, the hyperconnected architecture achieves a 95% fill rate and $33.6M profit versus 83% and $27.7M for the traditional architecture. Under compound disruption, the hyperconnected architecture remains within 1–2% of its own baseline profit across all scenario classes including catastrophic severity, while the traditional architecture loses up to 48% of profit under simultaneous two-node hazards. This work received the **3rd Best Paper Award** at IPIC 2026.
